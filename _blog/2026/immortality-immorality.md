During a conversation about immortality a friend placed an outstanding remark. We were discussing the implications immortality or even just a rather large extension of life-expectancy would have. Noting that immortality would not bring any new problems whatsoever, but instead magnify the visibility of many existing problems through increased impact and scale.

> See also the relevant Love Death and Robots episode ["Pop Squad"](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt10468230/)

Were a 100 or even 1000 year life extension (technology) invented we'd see enormous social turmoil. This could perhaps best be imagined as a combination between what we've seen when the first COVID vaccines were distributed, the conspiracy theories afterwards and the hype cycle propagated around AI all combined into one. It would certainly not be met with universal acclaim celebrating the great achievements of humanity. Instead there'd be worries about equality, impact on society, moral, and ethical dilemmas. Quickly we'd be met with two opposing camps, and perhaps a little in-between. There'd be the proponents, opponents, and a small group seeing the potential benefits but also recognizing how the newfound innovation is tainted.

The creation of new technology like this does not so much create new problems as much as it amplifies existing problems. Existing power structures get reinforced. Technology gets applied to satisfy the deepest desires and wishes of their consumers, or perhaps is used to address their deepest fears instead. Who are we fooling if we claim that humanity will have universal access to such thing? First people will make good money, and by extension it'd only be available to the most powerful on earth. Only by accident or markey dynamics will it become accessible to a broader public. If it were only socially accepted, slavery would still have been a thing. Who are we kidding to assume slaves would have their life extended, or even if it was, who are we kidding by suggesting they only have to serve for part of their life? If slaves can go free that suggests the social fabric is malleable which would be a threat to our wealth and power. No, no, no. We cannot have that.

Much of the same discourse can be seen around large language models, and in that regard I would encourage people to change their perspective to see how this technology satisfies peoples desires and how it answers their fears. How it entrenches existing power structures, and how it furthers inequality. We can be for or against some form of technology, but perhaps we'd better examine ourselves and how this answers the things we do not dare to speak out loud.
